How to Use

To use the evolver, input a Latin word in its modern scholarly orthography. The pronunciation will automatically be added when you submit. If it looks off, you may need to edit your input.

Accepted Latin orthography:

  • Macrons or acutes should be used to represent long vowels. Use precomposed characters only.
  • Breves may indicate short vowels, but are not recommended or required.
  • ⟨j⟩ and ⟨v⟩ are interpreted as merely orthographic variants of ⟨i⟩ and ⟨u⟩, and will not indicate or force a semivocalic interpretation.
  • Diaresis of ⟨ae⟩, ⟨oe⟩, ⟨au⟩ is not accepted, but ligature is.
  • Use a hyphen (-) to break up morpheme boundaries if it would change pronunciation.
    e.g. me-us or ad-iacēntem.
    It can also be used to mark a vocalic ngu sequence, as in unguō vs ung-uō.
    It can also be used to mark a semivocalic v, as in servientem vs ser-vientem.
  • Capitalization does not matter.
  • Remember that the vast majority of surviving Romance nouns come from the accusative case in Latin. If you don't want your masculine nouns to have an erroneous -s at the end, fix that.
